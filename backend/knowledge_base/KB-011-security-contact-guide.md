# Security Contact Guide

> **Simulated content.** Fictional sample documentation for a demonstration
> system. All contact details below are fictional placeholders.

## 1. Who to contact

| Situation | Contact | Availability |
| --------- | ------- | ------------ |
| Suspected phishing email, no interaction | Report Phishing button, or `phishing@example.com` | 24×7 (monitored in business hours) |
| Credentials entered on a suspicious page | Security hotline `+00-555-0100` (fictional) | 24×7 |
| Suspected malware or a strange device | IT service desk, then security hotline if urgent | Business hours / 24×7 hotline |
| Lost or stolen device | IT service desk **and** security hotline | 24×7 |
| Suspected data leak | Security hotline and `security@example.com` | 24×7 |
| VPN or connectivity problem | IT service desk | Business hours |
| Access request or role change | IT service desk self-service portal | Business hours |
| Physical security concern | Facilities and site security | 24×7 |

## 2. Out-of-hours and emergencies

Outside business hours, use the **security hotline**. Use it immediately for:

* credentials you believe are compromised;
* a device you believe is infected;
* a lost or stolen device containing company data;
* evidence that data has left the company;
* anything that looks like an active attack.

Do not wait until the next business day for these. Reporting late makes
containment harder and increases impact.

## 3. What to include in a report

A good report contains:

1. **What happened** — in plain language, one or two sentences.
2. **When** — date and local time, as precisely as you can.
3. **What you were doing** — which application, site or email.
4. **What you did** — did you click, type, open, download, approve?
5. **What you observed** — exact error messages, pop-ups, sender addresses.
6. **Your device and location** — for example "managed laptop, home network".
7. **Evidence** — keep the original email, take a photo of the screen, do not
   delete anything.

If you are unsure whether something is worth reporting: report it. The security
team would much rather triage a false alarm than investigate a breach that was
noticed a week late.

## 4. What the security team will never ask you for

The security team and the IT service desk will **never**:

* ask for your password;
* ask for a one-time MFA code;
* ask you to approve an MFA prompt;
* ask you to install remote-access software from a link they send by chat;
* ask for payment, gift cards or cryptocurrency;
* ask you to keep an investigation secret from your manager.

Anyone who does any of these things while claiming to be from IT or security is
an attacker. Verify by calling the service desk on the published number, then
report the contact attempt.

## 5. Response expectations

| Report type | Acknowledgement target |
| ----------- | ---------------------- |
| Credentials compromised, malware, data leak | 15 minutes (hotline), 24×7 |
| Suspected phishing with interaction | 4 business hours |
| Phishing report without interaction | 1 business day |
| General security question | 1 business day |

You will receive an incident reference number. Keep it for follow-up.

## 6. Privacy

Reports are handled confidentially. Reporter identity is recorded in the
incident ticket and is visible only to the security team. Reporting a mistake
you made is always treated as a positive contribution, never as a disciplinary
matter.
